The party is short one dungeon master.

Mythrun runs the table. It builds the world, plays everyone in it, calls for the rolls, and remembers what happened three months ago. Two to five players, fifth edition, and nobody has to give up their week to prepare.

What Mythrun does

The world

You describe a place in a paragraph. Mythrun writes the rest. The towns, the roads between them, the people who live there, and the quiet trouble they are already in. Nothing runs on rails. There is no plot to spoil, because none was written in advance.

The table

It narrates. It voices the innkeeper, the customs officer, the thing in the cellar. It calls for the check and reads back what the dice gave you. Combat moves to a map and runs on fifth edition rules. Dice are rolled in code, never invented.

The memory

Between sessions the world keeps its own notes. What you burned stays burned. The debt you left in the last town is still owed when you come back in March, and the person you owed it to has had time to think about it.

What it costs

Two things cost money. Starting a world, and playing a session. Joining someone else's campaign, making a character, and reading back your own transcripts cost nothing.
