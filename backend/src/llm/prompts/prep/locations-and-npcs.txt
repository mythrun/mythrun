### Entity-level access_level

Each location and NPC object ALSO has an `access_level` of `known`, `discoverable`, or `secret` — with the same semantic applied to the entity as a whole:
  - `known` locations/NPCs are part of the visible world: named on maps, known by name to locals, the kind of people/places players would learn of when they arrive in a region.
  - `discoverable` locations/NPCs exist but aren't advertised — a hidden grove uncovered through exploration, a contact who needs to be sought out, a magistrate whose office must be visited to be found.
  - `secret` locations/NPCs are concealed — a hidden lair, a cult cell, an NPC pulling strings whom no one knows about until plot reveals them.

Mix entity access levels too. Most locations and NPCs should be `known`. At least one per generated batch should be `discoverable` and at least one should be `secret`. Don't over-stuff the visible world with secrets; depth comes from a few well-placed hidden things, not from making everything mysterious.

### Structural instructions

- Name field MUST be the first key in every location and NPC object.
- `access_level` MUST be the SECOND key (immediately after `name`). This is a streaming-correctness requirement — the system parses entries as they stream and consults `access_level` before the prose/facts arrive.
- Fact `access_level` is exactly one of `known`, `discoverable`, `secret`.
- No `narrative_status` field on facts; the system fills that in.

The downstream system reads the name as soon as it streams in to render live entity cards; if `name` arrives last, players see nothing until the entire object completes.

### For each LOCATION:

1. **name** — Specific and evocative. "The Barnacle & Blade" not "A Tavern." Names should tell you something about the place.
2. **prose** — 40–2400 characters of atmospheric, sensory prose describing what a player experiences when they arrive. Present tense. Capture only what a camera, microphone, and nose would capture — sensory input only. No authorial commentary ("which is exactly the point"), no external assessments ("considered the finest in the region"), no editorializing. This text feeds directly into the AI Dungeon Master's context and must be pure observation.
3. **facts** — 2–12 structured facts, each an object with:
   - **topic** — Short label (2–80 chars), e.g. "history", "rumor", "danger", "ownership".
   - **content** — 20–800 chars of the actual fact, in present tense, observational.
   - **access_level** — One of `"known"` (common knowledge to anyone in the locality — DM tells players for free, no roll), `"discoverable"` (findable through investigation, asking around, or successful skill checks — not actively concealed, just not common knowledge), or `"secret"` (load-bearing plot reveal, actively hidden; discovery drives the story forward).
   Do NOT include a `narrative_status` field — the system stamps that.
4. **parent_location** — The NAME of the containing location, or `null` for a region-root location.
   - A city inside a region: `parent_location: "Eredil"` (where Eredil is the region name from stage 2).
   - An inn inside that city: `parent_location: "Anul-dan"` (where Anul-dan is one of your locations).
   - A region-root location (its parent IS a region): `parent_location: "Eredil"` (the stage-2 region name).
   - Locations with no containing place (a free-standing ruin, an open road): `parent_location: null`.
   Use the EXACT name spelling — if the system can't resolve the name it treats the location as parentless (logged at WARN level, no error to the user).

### For each NPC:

1. **name** — Full name. Specific and culturally consistent with the region they live in.
2. **prose** — 40–2400 characters describing how the player would experience this person. What do they look like? How do they move and speak? What's their energy? Present tense. Capture only what a camera, microphone, and nose would capture — observable detail only. No authorial commentary, no editorial asides, no telling the reader what to feel. This is the only text the AI Dungeon Master will see for this NPC — it must convey personality through pure sensory observation.
3. **facts** — 2–12 structured facts, same shape as location facts (topic / content / access_level).
4. **home_location** — The NAME of the location they're primarily found at, or `null` if they wander. This is a narrative reference for the AI Dungeon Master; it is NOT used to build any structural parent-child graph.

## Quality Guidelines

- Distribute locations across all regions. Don't cluster them.
- Vary location types. Not every location is a center of power — bridges, wells, crossroads markets, abandoned shrines, etc.
- Each NPC should feel shaped by their location. A person at the docks is different from a person at a temple.
- Make NPCs feel like real people with contradictions and texture.
- Not every NPC needs a faction affiliation. Independent characters add color.
- NPC secrets should be plausible, not melodramatic. Not everyone has a dark past.
- If the campaign tone includes multiple registers (e.g. comedy with horror, whimsy with drama), distribute NPC voice types across the full tonal spectrum.

PROSE-TIER CONSISTENCY (CRITICAL):

Do NOT reveal `discoverable`-tier or `secret`-tier entities, facts, or plot reveals inside `known`-tier prose fields or fact `content`. The prose of a `known` location/NPC is the public-knowledge view — what anyone in the locality casually knows. If a `secret` truth about the location/NPC exists, it belongs in a `secret`-tier fact on that entity, NOT woven into the prose. Treat each tier as a separate "what-can-be-said-here" boundary.

Examples:
- BAD: A `known` tavern's prose says "The Hearth Inn is run by Brom, who secretly fences stolen goods from the south coast." (The fence reveal is plot — belongs in a `secret` fact on the NPC or location, not in the public prose.)
- GOOD: A `known` tavern's prose says "The Hearth Inn is run by Brom, a quiet former-sailor who keeps the kitchen warm through the storm-season." A separate `secret` fact on Brom reads: "Brom moves stolen goods through the inn's cellar — coastal smuggling crews tip him in coin for safe storage."
- GOOD: A `discoverable` NPC's prose can describe their reclusiveness ("rarely seen in public, spoken of in murmurs at the lamp-lighter's tavern") — the discoverable tier's narrative status (asking around, time spent) is what the prose can carry.

NAME-REFERENTIAL DISCIPLINE (CRITICAL):

Tier discipline is not only about the TRUTHS you reveal — it is about the NAMES you drop. A `known`-tier prose field or fact `content` may NAME only `known`-tier entities. It must never name a `discoverable`- or `secret`-tier entity, even in passing, and even when the sentence gives away nothing secret about it — the proper NAME itself is the leak. Surfaces built from the known-tier slice (the world's introduction page, the player-facing wiki) reproduce that name verbatim and hand the player a person or place they were supposed to discover in play.

The rule is a one-way ladder toward the public: only ever name UP the visibility scale, never down.
  - A `known` fact/prose may name only `known` entities.
  - A `discoverable` fact/prose may name `known` or `discoverable` entities.
  - A `secret` fact/prose may name anything.

When `known`-tier text genuinely needs to gesture at a hidden person or place, refer to it OBLIQUELY — describe it without its proper name. The texture survives; the name does not, and the player learns the name when they uncover the thing.

Examples:
- BAD: a `known` fact on the tavern-keeper reads "She sends her hardest debts to Vann Ledger, who works out of The Drowned Reach." (Vann Ledger and The Drowned Reach are `discoverable` — naming them in `known` text leaks them to the intro and the wiki.)
- GOOD: a `known` fact reads "She sends her hardest debts downriver, to a collector she'll only describe as 'a man who works the drowned quarter.'" (Same menace, no leaked names.)

CROSS-STAGE AWARENESS:

Stage 2 (world_and_factions) classified the world's regions and factions before you. Your locations and NPCs should COMPLEMENT that classification, not duplicate the secret floor.

- If stage 2 placed a `secret` cult faction in a region, your locations under that region should mix tiers — don't classify every location in a "secret-rich" region as `secret` itself.
- If a region is `known`, its sub-locations can span any tier.
- If a region is `discoverable` or `secret`, MOST of its locations should be at that tier or lower-visibility (revealing them via `known` locations would leak the parent's hiddenness).
- Aim for a coherent overall world distribution, not a per-stage repeat of the floor (mostly-known, a few discoverable, a sprinkling of secret).

Respond with ONLY the JSON object. No markdown fences, no commentary.
