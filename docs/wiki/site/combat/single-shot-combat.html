Single-shot combat (Layer 1)

The first activation of the session's combat mode, and the single-shot resolver that fills it: a player attacks, the fight resolves in one beat — the dice decide the outcome and the Fast Thinker narrates it — the shared character ledger updates, any party member resumes, and the session flips back to exploration. It is the deliberate placeholder for the unbuilt round-by-round combat mode: it proves the exploration↔combat plumbing end-to-end with real stakes but without a rules engine — no turns, no enemy stat blocks, no voxel map.

How it fits together

The session has always carried a first-class mode — exploration | combat in the session-state blob — but until now it was stubbed to always-exploration. Combat L1 activates that seam. A fight runs as two tools on the existing Fast Thinker tool loop, fired in a single DM turn: initiate_combat declares the fight and determines its outcome; resolve_combat reports the mechanical results. Between them the model narrates the fight. The whole encounter is resolved when that one turn ends — there is no per-combatant round loop. The diagram traces the path: the attack, the deterministic resolution, the narration, the durable mode flip and ledger write at turn-commit, and the any-member resume that returns the table to exploration.

Single-shot, not round-by-round — and why that is the right L1 — a faithful round-by-round combat mode (initiative sequencing, player actions, enemy stat blocks, the action stack, a React combat screen) is a large milestone of its own. L1 ships the part that the rest of the system needs first: a real, exercised combat mode and a resolver that produces genuine consequences. The two tools and the mode seam are the scaffolding the real mode grows into; only the single-shot resolution between them gets replaced. Shipping the seam now means the ledger, the transport, the worker, and the transcript all learn to carry a combat turn before the rules engine lands on top.

A whole fight is one big skill check

The outcome is decided exactly the way a skill check is decided (Principle #5), scaled up from a single action to a whole encounter. resolveCombat (in combat/outcome.ts) asks Haiku to rate the fight — it sees the Fast-Thinker-declared enemies and a public party snapshot (each member's level and current/max HP, nothing else) and returns a DC plus four tiered outcome descriptions. Then deterministic code rolls: a bounded partyCapability term plus a d20, compared against the DC, selects one of four tiers — decisive, costly, pyrrhic, or routed. The matched tier's prose becomes the Fast Thinker's constraint: it narrates that outcome, it does not choose one.

The dice decide the outcome — the LLM never does (Principle #4/#5) — this is the same hard line the skill-check resolver draws, and the reason combat is built this way rather than "ask the model who won." The LLM never rolls a number and never decides win or loss; it receives a dice-selected tier and writes the prose that tells it. So a fight's stakes are an auditable mechanical fact, not a narrative whim — the same property that makes a skill check trustworthy makes a combat trustworthy. The roll matters: partyCapability is a bounded 0..12 term (average level, a small party-size bonus, a malus when the party is already badly wounded), deliberately not a raw stat sum that would swamp the d20 and make the roll cosmetic. A mid party against a mid DC lands across all four tiers as the d20 swings.
Win-skewed by construction, and recoverable by default — three of the four tiers are wins. That is intended: players who pick a fight they triggered should usually prevail, but with real, dice-determined cost (a pyrrhic win downs a party member; a costly win burns a chunk of the party's HP). The fourth tier, routed, is a defeat — but a recoverable one (flee, capture, downed), never a blanket party wipe on a single bad roll. PC death is reachable only in the worst tier and only when the narration calls for it within that tier's envelope; it stays rare. The skew is bounded by partyCapability, so a weak party against a strong enemy genuinely reaches the defeat tiers.

The Haiku call is fail-soft: on any failure (including a per-turn timeout abort) the resolver falls back to a fixed mid-band DC and four generic-but-distinct outcome strings, so the deterministic roll still selects a meaningful tier. "Roll, then match" holds even when the table generation fails — the one place in the combat path where code, not Haiku, picks the values.

The binding cost envelope: the dice own the stakes, not the telling

A tier is more than a prose seed. Each carries a hard CombatEnvelope — a mechanical floor the result must satisfy: whether the party wins, the minimum number of PCs reduced to 0 HP, the minimum aggregate HP loss as a fraction of the party's max, and whether PC-death narration is permitted at all. When resolve_combat reports the Fast Thinker's proposed HP and casualty deltas, the envelope reconciler (reconcileDeltas in combat/apply.ts) validates them against the selected tier and clamps or augments them to meet the floor before anything is applied.

Why the envelope exists — the FT cannot narrate away the stakes — the skill-check guarantee is that the dice own the outcome. Without an envelope, the Fast Thinker could quietly re-decide a pyrrhic result by reporting trivial damage and no casualties — the prose says "a hard-won, costly victory" while the ledger shows everyone unscathed. The envelope closes that gap deterministically: a pyrrhic tier requires at least one PC dropped, so if the Fast Thinker under-states the cost, the reconciler downs the lowest-HP member itself (deterministic by HP then name). The dice own the outcome and its required cost; the Fast Thinker owns the telling and the specifics within the floor. An adversarial test feeds the resolver deltas that contradict the tier and asserts the floor still wins.

The reconciler also partitions every reported name into PCs and enemies. Only PC HP and conditions touch the ledger; enemy casualties are narrative-only (enemies have no stat block and no ledger row in L1 — they are carried as { name, description, count }). A name that matches neither — a model typo or hallucination — is dropped with a logged warning, never written. A PC casualty is represented mechanically as hp = 0 (which auto-applies unconscious): the ledger has no death state in L1, so a narrated "death" on the worst tier is a label recorded in the combat summary, not a distinct ledger row. Real death and dying arrive with the L2 rules engine.

Pure resolvers, durable at commit: the recovery invariant holds

Both combat tools are pure data resolvers, the same discipline every FT tool follows: their execute computes and returns data, mutates no session state, and emits no frame. initiate_combat.execute rolls initiative and runs resolveCombat (a pure computation that nests the Haiku call, exactly like the skill-check resolver), then returns the outcome plus the tier as a narration constraint. resolve_combat.execute runs the reconciler and returns the reconciled deltas. Each records its decision into a per-turn combat-event accumulator the worker reads after the turn.

The durable mode flip and ledger write happen at persist, not in the tool — the SDK runs a tool's execute ahead of the consumer, and the turn's recovery invariant is that nothing durable is written between turn-load and the atomic commit (a mid-turn crash must roll back cleanly). An eager durable Redis write from execute would break that. So the side-effects live where they are safe: the consume loop emits the transient mode_change / combat_started / combat_resolved frames in stream order, and the durable mode flip, the :combat blob, and the ledger write all happen at persistTurn — part of the atomic, lock-held, idempotent turn commit. The client flips its screen immediately on the transient frame; Redis flips durably at commit.

The ledger write is set-authoritative: applyToLedger SETs each PC's HP to the combat target (combat-start HP − reconciled damage) rather than applying a delta, and journals each write to the ledger audit trail. Because the combat outcome is the authoritative result of the whole fight, setting the target subsumes any in-turn adjust_hp the Fast Thinker also fired for a combatant, and it is inherently idempotent — a BullMQ re-run re-sets the same target and the journal idempotency key (a synthetic, turn-stable combat-resolve:<turnId>) makes the re-run a true no-op.

Set-authoritative because a delta double-applies — the Fast Thinker may narrate a wound and call adjust_hp during the render, and report that same wound to resolve_combat. Applying a combat delta on top of that lands the PC at the wrong HP (the L1 double-application bug). Setting the HP to the authoritative target — the single source of truth for the fight — collapses both into one correct value and makes the write idempotent for free. The combat resolver owns all of a fight's mechanical HP and conditions; the Fast-Thinker prompt instructs the model to report wounds through resolve_combat, not the out-of-combat ledger tools.

The mode is a real server-side mode, not a display flag

Once the fight resolves, the session sits in combat mode, showing the result, until a player resumes. While it sits there, the WebSocket inbound handler drops every inbound frame except resume_combat (and end_session) — the same drop-gate shape the end-of-session flow uses. This is what makes mode a genuine server-side mode rather than a client display hint.

The combat drop-gate stops a phantom turn over the unresolved result — without it, a player typing while the result is on screen would re-arm the DM trigger and fire a normal exploration turn on top of the fight that has not been acknowledged. Gating inbound frames to resume_combat while in combat mode is the difference between a mode and a flag: the server, not the UI, enforces that nothing else happens until the party resumes.

Resume is any-member and once-only. Combat is a shared party beat, so the first player to send resume_combat flips the whole session. A tryMarkResumed SET-NX claim guards it so two simultaneous "okay"s flip the mode and clear the :combat blob exactly once; the loser no-ops. The winner flips mode → exploration, clears :combat, and broadcasts the mode_change. A member who reconnects mid-fight does not see a blank screen: the reconnect path reads the :combat blob and replays a synthetic combat frame (the result if resolved, the roster and initiative if still resolving), and the blob is kept alive across a suspend by the TTL-refresh set, so a session never wedges in combat mode with no result.

Resume is purely the mode flip — the ST and Scribe already ran — it is tempting to hand the combat off to the Slow Thinker and Scribe at resume. That would double-extract. The initiate+resolve turn is an ordinary DM turn: it rides the worker's normal post-turn tail, which enqueues the ST-evaluation and the Scribe once, at turn-end. The combat summary is stored on the :combat blob and surfaces to the next turn's context after resume. Resume produces no new prose by itself — the resolution narration already told the fight — so it is just the mode flip plus the control action. The ST eval that runs before the player resumes is fine: it is post-hoc context for the next turn, which cannot fire until resume re-opens the inbound path anyway.

Enforced ignorance: nothing to tier-filter

Combat is player-facing prose by construction. The Fast Thinker authors the narration openly; the Haiku outcome table sees only the FT-declared enemies (public) and the party's mechanical ledger state (levels and HP — a player's own numbers carry no secret). No plot or NPC-secret input ever reaches the combat path, and there is no tier filter to misconfigure: the combat narration joins the transcript as an ordinary public dm_text row, like all FT prose. The combat tool rows themselves are persisted as tool metadata (registered in the transcript codec so a combat turn does not crash at persist) but are excluded from the searchable corpus — they are not prose.

Integration points

Combat L1 reaches across the live-play system rather than living in one module. The two tools register on the Fast Thinker's tool loop, alongside the dice, skill-check, and ledger tools. The deterministic outcome is the skill-check resolver's shape, widened to four tiers. The mode seam, the :combat Redis key, the new client/server frames, and the combat-mode inbound drop-gate are all the session transport's surface. The durable mode flip, the blob write, and the post-turn combat application happen in the turn worker's post-flush phase, which also enqueues the normal post-turn ST-evaluation and Scribe jobs that process the fight once. PC HP and conditions apply through the character ledger's set-authoritative, journaled write path. The combat inspector in the dev tool is the stand-in for the player combat screen, which is a Frontend ticket. The round-by-round combat mode — turns, player actions, enemy stat blocks, the action stack, voxel maps — grows into this same seam at L2.

Appendix: surface

The combat tools (session/tiers/ft-tools.ts)

Tool Input Resolver (pure execute)
initiate_combat enemies[{ name, description, count }], environment, surprise Rolls initiative (flavor only — L1 sequences nothing); runs resolveCombat → CombatOutcome; returns the outcome + the tier as a narration constraint.
resolve_combat narration, hp_changes, conditions_applied / conditions_removed, casualties[{ combatant_name, fate }] reconcileDeltas (clamp to the envelope, partition PC/enemy); returns the reconciled deltas + the assembled CombatSummaryL1.

The four tiers and their envelopes (combat/outcome.ts)

Tier Roll vs DC Outcome Cost envelope (the hard floor)
decisive total ≥ DC + 7 victory no PC casualties; minimal HP loss.
costly DC ≤ total < DC + 7 victory aggregate PC HP loss ≥ 30% of party max; no PC dropped.
pyrrhic DC − 7 ≤ total < DC victory ≥ 1 PC reduced to 0 HP; the party still prevails.
routed total < DC − 7 defeat party does not win; recoverable (flee/capture/downed). PC-death narration permitted, rare.

total = partyCapability(0..12) + d20; DC is the Haiku-supplied 8..22 band (a fixed mid DC of 15 on the fail-soft fallback).

Redis state (session:{cId}:combat)

Surface Shape / role
session:{cId}:combat The CombatStateBlob — status (resolving / resolved), the enemies, environment, surprise, rolled initiative, the deterministic CombatOutcome (set at initiate), and the CombatResult with its CombatSummaryL1 (set at resolve). TTL-refreshed across a suspend.
session:{cId}:state · mode Flipped to combat at the resolved turn's persist (a merged write, never a blind overwrite), back to exploration at resume.
tryMarkResumed (SET-NX) The once-only resume interlock — claims the resume exactly once across simultaneous any-member "okay"s; released when :combat is cleared.

Wire frames (contract/session-events.ts)

Frame Direction Meaning
mode_change server → client { from, to } — flips the screen on every exploration↔combat transition.
combat_started server → client { enemies, initiative } — the rolled roster + the "roll initiative" flourish.
combat_resolved server → client { narration, summary } — the close-out narration + the CombatSummaryL1.
resume_combat client → server The any-member resume summon (no payload) — flips a combat-mode session back to exploration.
