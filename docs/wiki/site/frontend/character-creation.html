Character-creation experience

What a player walks through between the finished world and the first scene of play: the owner picks an opening situation and a party-wide starting level, everyone makes a character — either by describing them in a sentence or by answering a few D&D questions — a 1-bit "coming into being" beat holds the screen while the sheet is generated, the player meets their character as a field-guide entry and confirms it, and the owner begins the adventure. The whole arc turns on one idea: the frontend decides which screen to show from viewer-relative signals — the campaign status, the current user's own concept status, and whether they own the world — never from the derived pipeline phase. A DOM-free client-core brain holds the flow logic; @mythrun/ui paints it; discrete routes and one campaign-page surface share the work.

How it fits together

The experience follows the frontend's brain/render split (see the design system). The client-core/character subpath is the platform-neutral brain — the create-flow state machine, the guided-builder reducer and its flatten-to-concept-text fold, the sheet and party-roster view-models, and the per-user SSE fold behind the loading beat — none of it touches the DOM. A sibling client-core/srd subpath carries the static SRD-2014 catalog the guided builder shows. @mythrun/ui turns the brain into pixels: a level card, a freeform prompt, a guided builder, the loading beat, the sheet reveal, and the party roster. @mythrun/web composes them across a handful of discrete routes and one campaign-page surface. The routes consume five backend surfaces — the situations/level reads, the concept submit, the status SSE stream, the character-creation progress board, and the finalize fan-in — and never reach past a client-core function to do it. The backend pipeline that generates each sheet is a separate page; this one is the flow, the screens, and the routing model.

Two state models, one flow

There are two distinct state models in play, and keeping them apart is what makes the flow both smooth and reload-proof. The first is the create-machine (client-core/character/create-machine.ts): a tiny in-session machine — chooser → freeform | guided → processing → reveal → staging — that sequences the create route's own screens while the player is actively composing a character. The second is the routing table (deriveCharacterRoute): a pure function that maps three durable, server-backed signals onto a screen, re-evaluated every time the campaign page mounts.

Why two models instead of one — the create-machine is ephemeral: it lives in React state, remembers which path you chose and whether you have submitted, and vanishes on reload. That is exactly right for a form you are filling out, and exactly wrong for the question "where is this player in the arc?" — which has to survive a refresh, a second browser tab, and the ten minutes a character sheet takes to generate. So the durable question is answered by re-reading server state (the concept status, the confirmation timestamp) on every mount, not by trusting an in-memory machine. The create route drives the machine; the campaign-page surface drives the table; the two never share state.

The routing model — viewer signals, not the pipeline phase

This is the load-bearing decision. The frontend does not route on the derived pipeline phase. That phase is the wrong signal for three concrete reasons: derivePhase returns character_creation only transiently while a concept is processing and falls back to world_ready the moment concepts are completed; it collapses any one member's failed concept into a party-wide failed phase; and the status client further coarsens that into a terminal state that short-circuits to the world-gen TerminalView before any per-user branch runs. Routing on it would dead-end a recoverable concept on the wrong screen and let one player's bad roll terminal-screen the whole table.

Three viewer-relative signals instead — the surface reads campaign.status (which stays a stable setup throughout creation), the current user's own character_concepts.status (from GET /character-creation-progress, found by matching the viewer's id), and the viewer's role (CampaignSummary.creator === null ⟹ owner). All three are relative to the person looking: a member and the owner viewing the same campaign at the same instant can, and should, land on different screens. This is why the failed-concept case shows a calm character-context retry rather than the world-gen terminal, and why a party never all-goes-home for one member's failure.

deriveCharacterRoute reproduces the spec's §3.3 table faithfully, all within campaign.status === 'setup' unless a terminal gate fires first:

Condition (for the viewing user) Screen
campaign failed (terminal integration failure) character-context failure + retry (not the world-gen terminal)
campaign active, or concept integrated play entry
no starting situation chosen owner → situation pick; member → hold
situation set, no starting level owner → level pick; member → hold
both set, no concept row (or a resubmittable one) create
concept failed character-context failure + retry (reset → create)
concept processing loading beat
concept completed, confirmed_at null meet your character
concept completed, confirmed_at set party staging (owner: finalize; member: waiting for the party)
confirmed_at is what makes the reveal survive a reload — "meet your character" and "party staging" are both completed; the only thing separating them is whether the player pressed This is me. Keying that split on an in-session flag would bounce a player back to the reveal on every refresh in the completed→active window. So confirmation is persisted (character_concepts.confirmed_at) and the progress board serves it back — a reload re-reads the timestamp and lands on staging, not the reveal, exactly as it should.

Discrete routes vs the campaign-page surface

The flow is split across two web layers, and which layer owns a beat is deliberate. The discrete routes (router.tsx) are the one-way "compose and submit" steps: /campaigns/:id/situation, /…/level, /…/character/new, /…/join, and /…/play. The campaign-page surface — CharacterSurface, mounted by CampaignPage at /campaigns/:id, superseding its old stub seam — owns the durable lifecycle beats: the member hold, the loading beat, the reveal, party staging, the character-context failure surface, and the play entry.

The two connect through the routing table. When the surface decides the viewer is an owner who still owes a situation, a level, or a first concept, it does not render those inline — it redirects to the matching discrete route (a react-router Navigate). The discrete route does its work and hands off: the situation pick POSTs the chosen index and navigates to the level route; the level pick POSTs 1–5 and navigates to /character/new; the create route runs the create-machine and submits. Once a concept exists, the durable states (processing → completed → confirmed → integrated) are read back at /campaigns/:id and the surface mounts the loading, reveal, and staging beats itself.

A refresh or deep-link of the create route hands off, never restarts (myth-qkjdq) — /character/new is for a fresh or reset concept. If a player reloads it — or follows a stale deep-link — while their own concept is already processing, completed, integrated, or failed, the route reads the roster (useViewerConceptStatus matches the viewer's fetchMe id against the progress board) and redirects to the campaign surface, whose routing table owns the waiting beat, the reveal, staging, the play entry, and the failure surface. Only none / submitted — a genuinely fresh or reset concept — stay on the create flow; a probe still loading or failing resolves to that same safe default, and the backend 409-guards a re-submit of a locked concept regardless. Without this a refresh mid-generation restarted a character the player had already committed.
The create-machine's tail states are vestigial — the machine can reach processing, reveal, and staging, but on the discrete /character/new route only chooser → freeform | guided → processing are actually driven; nothing there dispatches ready or confirm. On submit the route redirects to the campaign surface (/campaigns/:id, a replace so Back never returns to a spent form) — MYT-102 replaced the dead processing placeholder that used to sit here — and the real loading beat and reveal are the campaign-page surface's beats, keyed on the server concept status. The machine sequences the in-session form; the surface owns everything after submit. This is the same "durable question is answered by server state" split as above, seen from the route's side.

The owner's opening — situation, then starting level

Two owner-only beats precede character creation, because both decisions are party-wide. The situation pick (a modification of the shipped worldgen/situation-pick.tsx, now owner-gated) offers the world's three opening hooks — title, place, and a paragraph of prose — as a borderless GhostHookList. Each hook also carries an LLM-authored invitation whisper generated with it; the situation pick keeps it in the response and character creation consumes it later as the grounding prompt. The level pick offers five LevelCards (1–5), each with a plain, hedged one-line gloss of what the level grants. It pre-selects the party's already-persisted starting level when one exists — the owner arriving back via Back — and falls back to level 3 only on a first visit, so a re-confirm can never silently downgrade a chosen level (MYT-102 QA M1); confirming locks the party's shared starting level and hands off to create.

The situation pick reuses GhostHookList, not a new SituationCard — the design called for a dedicated card component, but the shipped pick composes the already-shipped ghost-hook list primitive instead. It reads correctly in the flat 1-bit register with zero new design-system surface. This is the "prefer a composition of shipped primitives" resolution the spec left open (§5.4): only pieces that are genuinely reusable become new DS components. The path chooser is the same call — an in-route composition of Card/Button, not a component.

Both beats fail closed. Role is resolved from the /api/campaigns/mine payload (creator === null ⟹ owner); an unresolved or failed probe resolves to member, so the owner-only picker never flashes for a non-owner. A member reaching either route directly sees a calm "setting up the table" hold. And each pick treats its own already-locked 409 (situation_locked / level_locked) as idempotent success — it just hands off — so a double-submit or a stale tab never strands the owner.

The two paths into a character

The create route opens on a path chooser: "tell us who you are" or "build your character." Both paths POST the same two-field body — a conceptText and a situationGroundingText — and both collect the grounding field the same way, through the chosen situation's invitation whisper.

The freeform path collects the two required fields one at a time: first "Who are you?" → conceptText, then the situation's invitation whisper → the grounding text. Each step now carries a quiet mono "N of 2" signpost above the prompt (MYT-102), so a player knows a second question is coming and doesn't front-load their whole character into the first answer. Each field mirrors the backend's 20-character floor as a calm inline nudge (never a red error), and a synchronous in-flight guard keeps a double-press from firing a second submit. The guided path is a controlled driver around the presentational GuidedBuilder, wired to the client-core guided reducer: one question at a time — Class → Subclass → Species → Background → Notes → grounding — with a progress rail carrying the prior picks. The reducer owns the sequence and the conditional skip: the Subclass step appears only when the party's starting level grants the chosen class its subclass (SUBCLASS_LEVEL[class] ≤ level), and stepping back over a skipped step just works. MYT-102 extracted the rail into a standalone GuidedRail with a first-class grounding rung, so the signposting stays on screen through the builder-less Notes and grounding screens instead of vanishing when the option grids do. On completion the reducer flattens the picks into an English concept sentence from the catalog's canonical names, and the grounding whisper collects the second field exactly as freeform does.

The flatten emits canonical English, not display copy — the concept text the backend LLM reads is composed from the SRD catalog's canonical English names ("I am a Fighter, a Champion. I am a Human with a Toll-clerk background."), because LLM input is locale-independent. The [ph:] display copy the cards show is a separate, later web-layer concern. The subclass clause is dropped from the sentence whenever the level does not grant it — the same gate the builder uses to skip the step — so a stale draft subclass can never leak into the concept.

Writing against the world — the recap and the wiki at hand

The playtest's core complaint was that a player wrote their character into a vacuum: the opening scene they were grounding against wasn't on screen, and the world they were joining was a name and a paragraph. MYT-102 puts both in front of them. A SituationRecap card — a quiet paper card with the chosen situation's title, its location, and its prose — sits above the free-writing prompts, so the player writes against the actual opening scene rather than from memory. In the guided path the recap appears only on the grounding screen, where the writing happens; on the mechanical option-grid steps (class, species, …) it would compete with the builder's own question, so it stays off them.

And the world's wiki is mounted alongside the whole create route, so a player can look up a place or a faction before they commit to how their character fits. The panel model was promoted out of the reveal into a shared routes/wiki-panel.tsx (the reveal keeps a behavior-preserving re-export shim): on desktop it is a collapsible ~30%-width side rail, default open, its model owned by the route so a collapse never refetches; on a phone it is the same edge-handle Drawer the reveal uses, flipped in on the lg breakpoint through useViewportBelowLg and mount-persistent so the reader's page survives a dismiss. Its data is the member-readable world snapshot — enforced ignorance is the server's concern, so the panel renders exactly the known-tier world the reveal does.

Coming into being — the loading beat

While the sheet generates, the surface mounts CharacterLoadingSurface over the CharacterLoadingBeat: the shipped 1-bit loading register (paper, not voxel — the April spec's voxel silhouette is post-launch), a full-width quantized ink meter under a column of surfacing beat rows — rebuilt on the shared BeatFigure engine, its dithered fill front advancing one decelerating step per completed beat (never a tweened bar) and shimmering in place between completions. Its real-time source is the MYT-85 status SSE, folded by useCharacterCreationStream and filtered to the current user's id — the stream is campaign-wide, so a party of four all watch the same channel, each seeing only their own stages. Under prefers-reduced-motion the fill snaps and the rows swap without churn.

MYT-102 made the wait honest and reload-proof, giving it the same responsiveness register world gen already had. The fold now seeds from the connect-time snapshot frame's per-user stages[] — so a mid-generation refresh rehydrates the already-completed stages instead of restarting the beats from empty — and live events fold monotonically on top (a stale buffered stage-started can't regress a snapshot-completed stage). The screen's advance no longer rides the SSE at all: a dedicated SSE-independent backoff poll of the progress GET (useCreationProgressPoll, myth-qkjdq) flips the viewer off their own terminal concept status — advancing to the reveal on completed and, newly, to the concept-failure surface on failed — so a missed completion event, or a stream that dies before it reaches the browser, can no longer strand the player on a dead screen. The live SSE fold still animates the beats; the durable poll is what guarantees the exit (resolved = done || poll.done || poll.failed). Beneath the rows an aria-hidden countdown ticks a ~N left estimate off the same stage-timings history the world-gen countdown reads, omitted entirely on a cold start (no history is not a fake instant estimate) and holding at its floor when a stage overruns. And once the elapsed wait passes 3× the first usable estimate, a calm stall notice appears, so a genuinely stuck job reads as "taking longer than usual" rather than as a frozen screen.

Snapshot-seeded, with a transport-independent poll as the durable exit — the live fold alone can't survive a refresh (the events it accumulated are gone) or a missed completion event (the terminal character_creation_complete arm is a live event, not replayed), and a campaign-status SSE can die at the CloudFront edge without ever reaching the browser. So the hook seeds from the connect-time snapshot for the beats and takes its advance from the durable progress board, not the in-memory fold: useCreationProgressPoll backoff-polls the same GET the finalize board reads (a jittered ~3–6s cadence, one in-flight probe at a time, the loop stopping the instant the concept reaches a terminal state) and latches done or failed off the viewer's own concept status. A returning player whose concept already completed is still routed straight to the reveal by the routing table above without ever mounting the beat; the poll is what keeps an in-progress refresh — or a live session behind a dead stream — from dropping to a dead loading screen. The screen advances whether or not a single SSE frame arrives.
The CloudFront-edge hypothesis was investigated and downgraded — the stall's first suspect was the SSE dying at the CloudFront edge, but the edge config proved streaming-correct. The durable fix isn't an edge tweak that would only narrow the window; it's the transport-independent poll, which makes the screen's advance immune to any stream failure by construction rather than betting on a healthy stream. Further hardening of the streaming path is tracked separately (myth-7ptok).

Meet your character

The reveal is a field-guide entry, not a stat block. CharacterRevealSurface fetches the finished character (the raw snake-case read), adapts it deterministically into a { curated, full } view-model — through the raw→curated adapter now promoted into client-core (MYT-94), so the route delegates to it and the in-session sheet reuses the same reshape — and renders CharacterSheetReveal: the name; a mono identity line; an AC / HP / Proficiency / Hit-Die / Speed stripe; six square ability tiles; one or two paragraphs of connection prose; and "what they can do / carry / cast" panels — the spells panel appearing only for casters. Three actions ride it: This is me persists confirmed_at and routes to staging; See full sheet is a pure client expansion of data already in props (skills, saving throws, the full kit — no network, no LLM call); and Start over calls the concept-reset route and returns to create (which does cost a fresh generation).

Every derivation on the reveal is deterministic math, never a prose parse — the adapter computes ability modifiers by formula, resolves saving throws through the SRD CLASS_SAVES map plus the structured proficiency bonus, and defaults an unreturned speed to 30 ft. Subclass is omitted rather than guessed: the character row has no structured subclass column, and splitting one out of the free class string or feature names is exactly the pattern-match ZFC forbids. The connection prose is assembled, not generated — the concept stage's own already-authored fields (physical description, personality sentences, the player's grounding text) concatenated in order, degrading to a shorter panel when a field is blank.

Party staging and Begin

Once the viewer's own character is confirmed, the surface mounts PartyStagingSurface over the PartyRoster. It fetches the roster from GET /character-creation-progress and maps it through the party-roster view-model (owner first, each row marked ready / creating / gathering / you). As of MYT-91 the roster is live: the shell's earlier blind poll is replaced by useLiveRoster (the campaign status SSE folds a teammate's character_creation_complete into an in-place refetch, with a window-focus + slow-poll fallback), and the invite affordance is the wired CampaignInvitePanel on the campaign page, not a no-op seam. In the solo case (launch-critical) the roster shows the one-person party plus a dashed invite slot and a "begin the adventure" control. In a party, the owner-only Begin is enabled once the owner's own concept is ready; it fans in every currently-completed concept through POST /finalize-character-creation, which triggers character integration, flips the campaign to active, and leads into the opening scene. Status reads as prose, never a badge or dot — the brand rule holds here.

The laggard boundary is a known limitation, not a bug — integration loads only the concepts that are completed at fire time, and the campaign then flips active, so a member who finishes after Begin is left out (a later finalize 409s not_in_setup). MYT-91 shipped the live roster and the invite/join flow, but re-integrating a late joiner into an already-active campaign remains a separate backend follow-up, deliberately not solved here. An integration failure routes to the calm character-context failure surface (retry re-attempts the fan-in), never the world-gen terminal.

The member's briefer way in

A joining member (arriving via an invite) skips the owner beats — the situation and level are already set — and lands on JoinSummaryRoute: a stripped-down reveal that fetches the world snapshot and shows just the world's name and a paragraph of introduction in the same paper/serif register, then a single "create your character" CTA into the same create route. The rest of the arc (loading, meet, staging) is identical; a member simply waits for the owner's Begin instead of pressing it.

The member summary reads the world-snapshot, and fails closed on anything else — the world-snapshot endpoint is readable by any accepted campaign member (myth-0xwos widened it from owner-only for exactly this render), so a joined member fetches the same known-tier intro the owner's reveal uses. A failed or forbidden fetch — a transient error, or a member whose invite isn't accepted yet — resolves to the same calm, retryable notice, never a fake, nameless world, so the surface is honest about a gap rather than papering over it. (The join-summary.tsx header comment still describes the pre-myth-0xwos owner-only state; the route guard is the source of truth, and it is member-scoped.)

The brain — the client-core character layer

The character data layer is a real MYT-90 deliverable: none of it existed before. The client-core/character subpath holds the create-machine, the guided reducer and its flatten, the sheet and party-roster view-models, and the per-user stream fold — all DOM-free, all unit-tested against injected inputs. The client-core/api layer adds the character-submit, progress, finalize, starting-level, reset, and confirm endpoint slices (each a defineEndpoint with Zod request/response validation), and the client-core/srd subpath carries the static SRD-2014 catalog the guided builder shows. Every @mythrun/ui character component takes its props from a DOM-free contract file in client-core/contract/character/ first — the enforced portability seam — so the components stay controlled and presentational, with all sequencing in the reducer.

The subclass table is one SRD fact kept in two places — the client-core SUBCLASS_LEVEL table (which gates the builder's Subclass step) and the backend's level-scaling prompt/validation are two independent copies of the same SRD-2014 rule, with no shared enforcement. A drift between them yields a concept the sheet stage can't satisfy coherently. Both are pinned explicitly (not derived) and unit-tested against the same 12-classes × 5-levels matrix, and the coupling is noted at both ends. The CLASS_SAVES map the reveal uses to compute saving throws is the same shape of client-side mechanics — the only mechanics in the SRD data, homed here because the reveal derives saves in deterministic code rather than reading them off the wire.

Operating this piece

Walk the character-creation flow locally

  1. Bring up the stack: pnpm db:up, pnpm dev (backend + worker), and the web app (pnpm --filter @mythrun/web dev). A real generation needs live Anthropic credentials in backend/.env.development.
  2. Sign in, create a campaign, and let the world generate. On the settled reveal, press Continue — as the owner you land on the situation pick, then the level pick.
  3. Choose a path on /campaigns/:id/character/new. Freeform collects two prompts; the guided builder walks Class → (Subclass) → Species → Background → Notes, then the grounding whisper. Submit.
  4. Return to /campaigns/:id to watch the loading beat fill in, then the reveal. Press This is me, then Begin the adventure to finalize and enter play.
  5. To inspect the underlying events, open the same campaign in the dev tool's wire-log drawer — it streams the identical SSE frames the loading beat is folding, including the per-user character_creation_complete arm.

Integration points

The flow reads the backend character-creation pipeline through the situations/level reads, the two-field concept submit, the character-creation progress board, and the owner finalize — the last of which triggers character integration and the flip to active. The loading beat rides the same status SSE transport as the rest of the app, filtered to the current player. The preceding world-gen reveal hands off here once a starting situation is chosen (campaign-detail.tsx delegates to CampaignPage), and the whole surface is built from mythrunUI components over the DOM-free client-core brain. Enforced ignorance and the sheet's mechanical honesty are the pipeline's concern; the frontend renders only the player-safe projection it is served.
