Campaign hub

The player's home base between sessions: a quiet shelf of their campaigns, each a small book-cover card carrying its world's colour identity, with a clear path to make a new one. The hub is the first real screen the app renders — a full-stack vertical from the /mine payload to the cover art.

How it fits together

HomeRoute owns a <Suspense> boundary; the data-consuming inner component calls useMyCampaigns — a client-core suspense query over GET /api/campaigns/mine — so it only renders once the payload resolves. A screen reaches the backend only through a client-core hook, never raw fetch; the loading spinner and the route-level error boundary handle the other two states. Above the boundary sits the page header row — the title and a filled New campaign button — rendered unconditionally, because starting a campaign is valid whether the shelf is loading, empty, or full. Below it, zero campaigns show the first-run empty shelf; otherwise the responsive body renders.

The shelf is ordered by activity

The hub's job for a returning player is "continue where you left off," so the body sorts before it renders. sortCampaignsForHub orders by most-recent activity — a campaign's activity time is when it was last played, falling back to when it was last touched for one never played — and the premier slot, the grid's top-left cell where the eye lands, belongs to whatever that puts first.

A pure, stable sort over a copy, never the cache's array — the payload arrives from a React-Query cache the hub does not own, so the sort returns a new array rather than mutating in place. Equal activity times keep input order (each entry carries its original index as the tiebreaker), so the shelf is deterministic across engines and re-renders instead of reshuffling under the player on an unrelated re-render. The earlier hub had no sort at all, which is how not-yet-started campaigns ended up sitting ahead of active ones in an arbitrary order.

The cover card — a book cover

Each campaign is a portrait CampaignCard, composed entirely from mythrunUI primitives — no ad-hoc UI. A CoverCard carries the world's two-colour identity as a full-bleed CoverDither band (~40% of the card), an ink seam, then a body: a serif title and an italic world · creator line at the top (suppressed when it would only echo the title — a campaign name defaults to its world name, so the sub-line renders only once a rename or a joined-world creator credit gives it something to add), and a mono "imprint" pinned to the foot — the party seals (square Avatar tiles) over the record/state line, closing on the card's one accent action line. The whole card is one link to the campaign's detail.

Four zones at fixed offsets, so a shelf of cards reads as a shelf — the card's pieces used to size to their own content, so a one-line title and a two-line title put their sub-lines, seals, and state lines at different heights, and a row of covers read as a ragged pile. Every zone now holds a fixed offset: the title is a two-line band (a clamp plus a reserved height in line-height units, so it tracks the type role rather than a magic pixel), the sub-line truncates to exactly one line, and a card whose state line has been promoted into the action zone keeps its ledger slot as a blank line rather than closing the gap. The premier card is the one deliberate break — it reads one type size up, which is the whole point of the slot.
The imprint reflects the party assembling (MYT-91) — the foot seals now distinguish a member who has a character (a pigment Avatar) from one still building their character (a muted placeholder seal — the Avatar variant that surface added to the design system). Both sit in one AvatarGroup, so the overlap ring and the +N overflow span the combined cluster; built seals lead so a ready party reads first, and the seal cap drops still-building members before built ones. The live-roster and invite story behind the imprint is the invites & party page's.
Status is text, never a dot — the paper register forbids status dots, badges, and chips. A campaign's state is conveyed by the mono ledger line plus the implicit party seals, and nothing else. The line is derived once by cardPhaseLine from the payload's cardPhase: "shaping your world", "make your character", "waiting for players", or, once played, the "N sessions · 2 days ago" record — the only place the hub formats a relative time (mechanical Intl.RelativeTimeFormat, locale-aware; the phrase is joined with non-breaking spaces so a count never wraps away from its unit at the card's narrow measure). The card and the mobile row render this exact string, so the logic lives in one shared module. MYT-95 adds one more foot line, still as prose: an owner-only low-credit nudge, gated on creator === null so a member never sees the owner's billing state (no member-visible owner-balance read exists, by design) and on the fail-open low-session-credits hook.

One action per card, at three weights

Every card closes on a single accent line naming what pressing it does, and the weight of that line is the card's whole status vocabulary. The premier card, when it has a play record, carries the loud continue session — the returning player's actual job, in the hero cell, with the promoted card treatment. Any other played campaign carries a quiet resume. A campaign still waiting on the viewer's character carries its needs-a-character line at a whisper, promoted out of the ledger slot into the action zone. Live and dormant are distinguished by the words and the weight, never by an action appearing and disappearing.

The action line is a label on the card, not a nested control — the whole card is already one link, so a real button inside it would be two overlapping targets with different meanings. The line names the card's action and the card is the target; there is exactly one thing to press. That is also why a cold-start campaign's cue whispers: a card the player has never opened must not out-shout one they have twenty sessions invested in, but it still has to be findable when scanning, so it keeps the accent and loses the weight.
The cover link is unstyled — mythrunUI's Link tints its whole subtree with the accent foreground, so a plain link would render the cover's title, world, and state in link-accent instead of ink. The unstyled variant drops the accent paint while keeping the accessibility focus ring, so a whole-card link still reads in the quiet paper-ink register. When a world has no colour pick yet (worldColors === null), the cover shows CoverDither's muted paper ghost band rather than borrowing a fake chromatic identity.

Grid on the wall, index in the hand

The card and the mobile row are visually distinct layouts — a cover card versus a table-of-contents line — so CampaignHubBody is a breakpoint switch, not a reflow. It uses the Show primitive (the one place a raw breakpoint class is legal, so the screen stays ad-hoc-UI-free): at and above sm, a fluid Grid of cover cards; below it, a compact Stack of hairline-divided index rows.

A fluid shelf, not a fixed column count — the grid uses auto-fill at the card's ~230px cover width, packing as many covers per row as comfortably fit, then wrapping. The shelf grows with the viewport instead of snapping to 1/2/3 columns. This needed the design system's new px minColumnPx track, since the token space scale caps well below a card width. Each mobile CampaignIndexRow is a small self-sized world swatch plus three truncating text lines beside it, so every row is exactly one line tall — uniform height by truncation, not a hard-coded pixel height.

A dashed CoverCardAdd tile trails the grid as a peer cell — the design-tool convention — so the shelf terminates in a natural "one more," wrapped in the same unstyled Link the cover cards use. On the mobile index there is no tile: the header button is the create path there, and the list stays a clean table of contents.

Two create affordances on purpose, and neither in the premier slot — keeping both a toolbar button and an in-grid ghost tile is a validated pattern, and they do different jobs: the header button owns intentional create and its primacy, the trailing tile is in-grid discovery. Between them the create path is always near whatever the player is looking at — top of page or end of shelf — without either one being far away after a scroll. What the tile must not do is lead: a first iteration put it in the top-left cell, which spent the shelf's highest-value real estate on "make another one" and pushed the player's actual campaigns right and down. That cell is the premier slot, and it belongs to the campaign they came back for.

The empty shelf

With no campaigns the hub centers a single "Unwritten" cover: a Card frame="dashed" (the design system's new dashed variant, reading as an outline waiting to be filled) wrapping a muted paper ghost band — the same ghost a still-generating world shows — over an evocative one-line invitation and a "Create a campaign" CTA that routes into the campaign-creation flow. The card click and the CTA both point at real routes.

The header credits

The always-present app frame carries the live session-credit balance. HeaderCredits reads it through the useBalance suspense hook and renders it as a labelled mono readout — the label in the frame's quiet chrome type role, the number in chrome-strong, the bar's only accent — inside the shared HeaderSlot, which gives a suspense-reading header slot its own <Suspense> and its own error boundary. The app shell owns the frame's composition; this is the slot the hub's balance lives in. One instance mounts per frame arrangement (desktop and mobile clusters), each with its own testid.

The label names the readout — and its wording has moved once — the product has two currencies, and a bare number in the highest-traffic chrome on the site silently equalled the session bucket and taught every player the wrong model, so MYT-99 labelled the readout "session credits". The navbar redesign tersened the visible label to "Credits" as part of the bar's quieter chrome (final wording is flagged for the end-of-frontend copy pass); the readout still tracks the session bucket only, the label still leads the number and doubles as the readout's accessible name, and the billing surface is where both buckets are itemized and explained.
The credits slot degrades alone — a slow or failed balance fetch must never tear down the whole authed shell through the router error boundary. So the slot loads behind a small spinner and renders nothing on failure; the "Add credits" action beside it (an item of the header menu on phones) sits outside that boundary, so a player can still act even if the number didn't load. Both it and the header menu's Sessions & billing entry route to the real billing surface at /billing. The card's click target is /campaigns/:id — the phase-aware world-generation experience, which shows the live generation graph while a world is still building and the settled reveal once it's done.
The readout refreshes on navigation, not on every spender's good behaviour — the frame persists across routes, so a balance spent mid-route (ending a session debits server-side) survived as a stale number on the next surface: the hub would briefly show the pre-end count. The slot now invalidates the shared balance cache on every pathname change, which refetches in the background and updates the readout in place (suspense only ever gates the first load). One cheap GET per navigation buys "the header is never stale across a navigation" as a chrome-level contract, rather than depending on every credit-spending mutation site remembering to invalidate. The mount run is skipped — the suspense query is already fetching right then.

Integration points

The hub consumes the campaign hub payload (useMyCampaigns) and the balance endpoint (useBalance), both through the client-core data layer. Every visible piece — the CoverCard, CoverDither, Show, the dashed Card, the AvatarGroup seals — is a mythrunUI primitive. The cover colours come from the world colour identity. Clicking a card routes to /campaigns/:id, the world-generation experience — the live generation graph or the settled reveal, depending on where the world is.
