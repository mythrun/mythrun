Auth & Billing

How a player becomes a signed-in user, how that identity travels with their requests, how per-resource authorization gates protected routes, how their session credit is metered, and how Stripe fits in.

Seven pages in this section: Identity — the Cognito User Pool, the PKCE public App Client, the Hosted UI domain, and the Google + Discord OIDC providers that back federated sign-in; JWT middleware — the Fastify plugin family that validates Cognito access tokens at the edge, the boot-time public-route allowlist hook, the bypass mode for local dev, and the requireUser narrowing helper protected handlers reach through; Onboarding & invites — the first-login flow that creates the users row, the requireOnboarded gate that attaches a frozen request.dbUser and refuses suspended accounts, and the six invite routes that let a DM-owner pull others into a campaign by sharing a 12-character code; Access control & WS ticket — the per-resource preHandler factories (requireCampaignMember, requireWorldCreator), the boot-time allowlist that fails the deploy on a misconfigured wiring, and the single-use 30-second Redis ticket the SPA fetches before opening a WebSocket; Stripe contract — the closed enum of lookup_keys, the cached SDK client pinned to one API version, the read-only boot probe (prices + Customer Portal config) that fails closed if Stripe's catalog drifts, the module-level SKU cache the rest of the system reads, and the restricted-key boundary that makes price mutation impossible on the wire; Billing webhook — the encapsulated Stripe-webhook plugin with its raw-body parser, the api-version-gated dispatcher, and the eight per-event handlers that turn Stripe deliveries into Mythrun state (purchases, subscription state, audit feed, credit grants, refund clawbacks); and Billing routes — the seven HTTP routes that comprise the player-facing billing surface (skus, balance, subscription, history, checkout, portal, and the pending_if_incomplete tier upgrade).
