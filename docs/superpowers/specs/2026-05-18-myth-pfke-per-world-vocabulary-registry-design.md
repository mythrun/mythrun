# myth-pfke: Per-world vocabulary registry for distinctive voice and IP-drift defense

## Lead

Mythrun targets D&D-mechanics-on-any-story-shape: traditional fantasy, urban modern magic, sci-fi, food-themed political drama, post-apocalyptic Western, anthropomorphic animal fable, film-noir-inside-a-mind, regency romance with fae — the Dimension 20 catalog is the breadth target. Today's world-gen pipeline produces good content but has no mechanism to ensure each world has its own distinctive vocabulary. Two related symptoms result: (a) worlds with IP-adjacent concepts (the Veil & Chalk test case, an HP-mirror prompt) absorb the source franchise's coined neologisms verbatim — "Statute of Secrecy" × 4, "muggle" × 16, "Floo" × 9, "pure-blood" × 1 — which is a real commercial-IP exposure for a paid product; (b) more broadly, every world reaches for genre-default vocabulary (pantheon names from D&D defaults, calendar from real-world months, honorifics from "Lord/Lady") even when the world's premise would support something more distinctive. Both symptoms have the same root cause: the world's distinctive vocabulary isn't an explicit artifact the pipeline produces or constrains against.

This design adds a per-world **vocabulary registry** — an emergent glossary of 12-25 distinctive terms covering whatever domains each world's premise demands, with no pre-defined category slots — generated by a new stage 1.5 (`vocabulary_intake`) between current stage 1 (`concept_intake`) and current stage 2 (`world_and_factions`). The registry is persisted as `jsonb` on the world row, rendered as deterministic prose, and injected into the system prompt of every downstream stage (2-7). Stage 1.5 also identifies which existing-fiction franchises the world is adjacent to (Harry Potter, Star Wars, Forgotten Realms, etc.) using model judgment from training data — no static IP list to maintain. A second stage (`vocabulary_consolidation`) is **fully designed here but deferred to a follow-on bead** that activates when MYT-52 (the character-creation runtime) lands and provides a hook point — when built, it will append distinctive terms surfaced during world-gen and character backstories that the predictive stage 1.5 didn't anticipate. The registry is the data; the rendered glossary is the player-facing surface (exposed via the world-snapshot endpoint, rendered in the dev-tool world-snapshot panel, and consumed by the future multiplayer wiki UI).

IP-drift defense falls out as a consequence: each registry entry can declare `avoids: "muggle"` when it's a world-specific replacement for an IP-iconic neologism. Downstream stages see both the positive constraint ("use 'the unwitched'") and the avoidance reminder ("instead of 'muggle'"). The model's intrinsic knowledge of which words are iconic to which franchises does the IP-cluster identification; no static curated list. The same data shape supports a future backstop Haiku IP-scan and future Scribe runtime write-back without schema migrations.

## Trigger and motivation

### Direct evidence: Veil & Chalk

World gen against the prompt `"A magical world focused on a wizarding academy. There is also a non-magical world that is unaware of the existence of this magical world. Takes place in 2026."` (campaign `019e38f1-7733-77cc-b14e-2d5c275ca741`, world `019e38f1-7733-77cc-b14e-2d5bfe19a6d2`, named "Veil & Chalk") cleared concept_intake's paint-job-validator — the world's bones are genuinely original (Section 14 muggle-intelligence with original ethics, Hedgewise communitarian witches, Founders' Vault metaphysics with belief-feeds-wards, contemporary disclosure-politics frame). But downstream stages 2-7 pulled in HP's protected neologisms unmodified:

- `"Statute of Secrecy"` × 4 (verbatim JKR-coined term)
- `"muggle"` / `"muggles"` × 16 (JKR-coined; Warner Bros has actively enforced this trademark)
- `"Floo"` / `"Floo network"` × 9 (verbatim HP magical-transport term)
- `"pure-blood"` × 1 (JKR-coined slur)

No iconic HP nouns leaked (no Hogwarts / Quidditch / Dumbledore / Hermione / four-house names / Obliviator / Patronus / Horcrux). The Slow Thinker avoided every iconic *named* concept. But the *interface vocabulary* between the magical and mundane worlds is what leaked — and it's the most legally exposed surface for Mythrun-the-commercial-product (200-500 concurrent paid sessions at launch). Warner Bros' IP enforcement history on HP is aggressive; this is not a theoretical exposure.

### Broader motivation: every world deserves its own vocabulary

The IP issue is the load-bearing trigger but it's a specific case of a more general gap. A standard D&D world deserves its own pantheon, calendar, honorifics, historical eras, in-world oaths, and any other distinctive terminology its premise supports. A Crown-of-Candy-style world needs food-themed vocabulary across every surface (deities, social classes, even concepts like "the Sweet" instead of a default sun-god). A Mentopolis-style world needs personified-emotion vocabulary, not D&D-default pantheon vocabulary. A Starstruck-Odyssey-style world needs Force-equivalent + ship-class + planet-type vocabulary. The current pipeline produces these terms ad-hoc inside stage 2-4 prose but doesn't elevate them into a glossary — which both denies the player a visible artifact (a wiki glossary section) and risks inconsistent usage across stages.

This design treats the vocabulary registry as a **first-class world artifact**: generated deliberately, consumed by every downstream stage, surfaced to the player.

## Goals and non-goals

**Goals:**

1. Every world has a per-world vocabulary registry of 12-25+ distinctive terms covering whatever vocabulary domains its premise demands. No pre-defined category slots; the model identifies and fills domains emergently.
2. Each registry entry has a `{term, definition}`, optionally `{avoids, usage_notes, source, access_level}`. The `avoids` field is the IP-defense signal.
3. The registry constrains every downstream world-gen stage (2-7) via prompt injection in the system prompt.
4. A second pass (post-character-creation) appends terms surfaced during generation that the predictive pass didn't anticipate.
5. The registry is exposed via the world-snapshot endpoint, rendered in the dev-tool world-snapshot panel, and forward-compatible with the future multiplayer wiki UI.
6. The mechanism handles IP-adjacent worlds (Veil & Chalk and similar) without a static curated IP-neologism list — the model's training-data knowledge does the cluster identification.

**Non-goals (explicitly out of scope; tracked separately):**

- A static curated global IP-neologism list. The original bead proposed this; this design replaces it with model-driven identification per world. May be revisited as a build-if-needed bead if frontier-model knowledge gaps surface.
- Post-generation backstop Haiku IP-scan on stage outputs (bead step 4 — filed as future work; registry's `avoids` + `adjacent_franchises` fields are exactly what that scan reads).
- Structural-archetype rubric tightening in the paint-job-validator (four-houses / broomstick-sport-style overlap). Orthogonal prompt-engineering work; separate bead.
- Scribe runtime write-back during play. The data shape supports it; no live-play code in this scope.
- Multiplayer wiki UI glossary rendering. UX-spec concern; the world-snapshot endpoint exposes the data, the multiplayer-runtime ticket will render it.
- Registry size cap / eviction policy. Over a campaign's lifetime with Scribe runtime write-back, the registry could grow unbounded. Out of scope here; schema doesn't preclude adding a cap later.
- Keyword-blocklisting user input at the chat layer. Too brittle, ZFC-violating; the model translates via the registry instead.

## Schema

### Migration (next migration in sequence; placeholder `015`)

```sql
ALTER TABLE worlds ADD COLUMN vocabulary_registry jsonb NOT NULL
  DEFAULT '{"entries": [], "adjacent_franchises": []}'::jsonb;

ALTER TABLE worlds ADD CONSTRAINT worlds_vocabulary_registry_shape
  CHECK (
    jsonb_typeof(vocabulary_registry) = 'object'
    AND jsonb_typeof(vocabulary_registry->'entries') = 'array'
    AND jsonb_typeof(vocabulary_registry->'adjacent_franchises') = 'array'
  );
```

**Backfill:** existing worlds get the default empty registry. Cosmetic — their stages are already done; downstream stages don't re-run for completed worlds.

**No index.** Registry is read with the world row, never queried directly.

**Per-entry validation is application-layer** (Zod), not DB-layer. A Postgres CHECK on the inner shape of array elements would require a 30-line `jsonpath` expression — not worth the complexity. The application is the only writer.

### Zod schemas

```typescript
// backend/src/llm/schemas/vocabulary-registry.ts

export const VocabularyEntrySchema = z.object({
  term: z.string().min(1).max(80),
  definition: z.string().min(30).max(400),
  avoids: z.string().min(1).max(80).optional(),
  usage_notes: z.string().min(1).max(200).optional(),
  source: z.enum(['stage_1_5', 'consolidation', 'scribe_runtime']),
  access_level: z.enum(['known', 'discoverable', 'secret']).default('known'),
})
export type VocabularyEntry = z.infer<typeof VocabularyEntrySchema>

export const VocabularyRegistrySchema = z.object({
  entries: z.array(VocabularyEntrySchema).max(60),
  adjacent_franchises: z.array(z.string().min(1).max(80)).max(8),
})
export type VocabularyRegistry = z.infer<typeof VocabularyRegistrySchema>

// Stage 1.5 output: model returns this. Handler then writes the
// validated result wholesale to the registry.
export const VocabularyIntakeOutputSchema = z.object({
  entries: z.array(
    VocabularyEntrySchema.omit({ source: true, access_level: true }),
  ).min(0).max(30),
  adjacent_franchises: z.array(z.string().min(1).max(80)).max(8),
})

// Consolidation output: model returns NEW entries only. Handler then
// merges append-only into the existing registry; source='consolidation'
// is stamped by the merge code, not by the model.
export const VocabularyConsolidationOutputSchema = z.object({
  new_entries: z.array(
    VocabularyEntrySchema.omit({ source: true, access_level: true }),
  ).min(0).max(15),
})
```

The 60-entry cap on `VocabularyRegistrySchema.entries` accommodates stage 1.5 (≤30) + consolidation (≤15) + future Scribe runtime growth. Stage outputs are tighter (`max(30)` / `max(15)`) to constrain each individual call.

Schema cap vs prompt target: the stage 1.5 prompt requests 12-25 entries as a soft target (guides the model toward a useful density without forcing a hard floor); the Zod schema enforces `max(30)` as a defense-in-depth upper bound (models occasionally overshoot soft targets by 1-5 entries; we accept that). Similarly, the consolidation prompt requests 0-15 new entries; the schema enforces `max(15)`. A model returning 0 entries is always valid (degenerate worlds for stage 1.5; nothing-new-worth-glossary-izing for consolidation).

## Stage 1.5: `vocabulary_intake` (predictive pass)

### Purpose

Read the world's identity (concept + concept_intake's structured output) and produce the predictive vocabulary registry — distinctive terms the model can anticipate from the concept alone. Includes IP-cluster identification.

### Prompt template (`backend/src/llm/prompts/prep/vocabulary-intake.txt`)

Sketch:

```
You are a world-building assistant for a tabletop D&D 5e campaign that
can be set in any genre — traditional fantasy, urban modern magic, sci-fi,
food-themed political drama, post-apocalyptic, anthropomorphic animal fable,
film-noir, regency romance with fae, anything that supports D&D mechanics.

You have already distilled the concept. Your job now is to generate this
world's distinctive vocabulary — the terms that make THIS world feel
specific and distinct from generic genre defaults.

## Campaign Summary

- **Name:** {{worldName}}
- **Concept:** {{concept}}
- **Tone:** {{tone}}
- **Themes:** {{themes}}
- **Era:** {{era}}
- **Geography:** {{geographySummary}}
- **Politics:** {{politicalSummary}}
- **Key tensions:** {{keyTensions}}

## Your Task

### Part 1: identify adjacent franchises

Identify the existing-fiction franchises this world is most adjacent to —
Harry Potter, Star Wars, Lord of the Rings, the Marvel Cinematic Universe,
the Forgotten Realms / Eberron / Dragonlance / Greyhawk (all WotC-D&D
owned settings outside the SRD), Redwall, Dune, Game of Thrones, anything
else. Use your knowledge of fiction broadly. If the world isn't notably
adjacent to anything, return an empty list.

### Part 2: generate the world's vocabulary

Generate 12-25 entries covering this world's distinctive terms. NO
PRE-DEFINED CATEGORIES — cover whatever vocabulary domains this world's
premise demands. Examples drawn from the breadth of stories this format
can support:

- Crown of Candy: food-themed deities (the Bulb, the Sweet, the Sour),
  food-themed nations, food-themed magic terminology.
- Mentopolis: 1940s noir slang merged with personified-emotion entities.
- Starstruck Odyssey: Force-equivalents, named ship classes, alien species
  names that aren't Star Wars's species names.
- The Unsleeping City: NYC-flavor magical institutions (the Spirit of
  NYC, the Vox Populi), neighborhoods reimagined as supernatural
  jurisdictions.
- Burrow's End: animal-fable territory terms, predator-and-prey language.
- A Court of Fey & Flowers: fae court honorifics, flower-magic terminology.
- Veil & Chalk: HP-adjacent — "the unwitched" instead of "muggle", "the
  Concordat of Concealment" instead of "Statute of Secrecy", "the Ash
  Network" instead of "Floo network".
- A generic D&D fantasy: pantheon names that aren't Mystra/Bane/Selûne;
  calendar that isn't Forgotten Realms; faction names that aren't the
  Harpers / Zhentarim.

For each entry, populate:

- `term`: this world's term (1-80 chars). Specific, evocative, fits the world's tone.
- `definition`: a 30-400 char prose definition explaining the term's meaning,
  scope, and how it's used in context. The definition tells downstream
  prose-generators HOW to use the term correctly.
- `avoids` (optional): IF this term is replacing an iconic neologism
  from an adjacent franchise, populate this with the franchise's term.
  E.g., `avoids: "muggle"` when the term is "the unwitched". The avoids
  field is the IP-defense signal — populate it whenever applicable.
- `usage_notes` (optional, ≤200 chars): capitalization, register, or
  stylistic constraints. E.g., "always lowercase, never preceded by
  'mere'".

DO NOT generate entries for:

- D&D 5e SRD-canonical mechanics: spell names, class names, race names,
  magic schools, ability scores, saving throws.
- Generic fantasy nouns: wand, sword, magic, dragon, witch, wizard.
- Boring proper nouns that aren't worth a glossary entry: every NPC
  name, every minor village.

Quality bar: every entry should be something a player would benefit
from seeing in a world-glossary wiki section. Distinctive, defining,
worth remembering.

Respond with ONLY the JSON object matching this shape:

{
  "adjacent_franchises": ["string", ...],
  "entries": [
    {
      "term": "string",
      "definition": "string",
      "avoids": "string (optional)",
      "usage_notes": "string (optional)"
    },
    ...
  ]
}

No markdown fences, no commentary.
```

### Handler (`backend/src/llm/prep/stages/vocabulary-intake.ts`)

Mirrors the shape of existing stage-1 (`concept-intake.ts`). Single non-streaming `generateObject` call (Sonnet, per default #3), routed through the canonical `ai.ts` wrapper for repair-fallback. On success, applies `source: 'stage_1_5'` and `access_level: 'known'` to each model-emitted entry, then writes the full `VocabularyRegistrySchema`-shaped object to `worlds.vocabulary_registry`. Returns the usual `StageCostResult`.

Failure modes are standard pipeline retry classification (myth-uppw): terminal errors abort world-gen; retryable errors retry; empty `entries` + empty `adjacent_franchises` is a valid success.

Cost: ~5K input tokens (concept + identity + diverse examples in the prompt template) + ~2K output tokens at Sonnet rates ≈ **$0.045/world**.

### Wiring into `world-gen-processor.ts`

Stage dispatch adds a new arm after `concept_intake` and before `world_and_factions`. New `PipelinePhase` arm `vocabulary_intake` between current `world_gen` and `world_snapshot_ready`. Pipeline-events / pipeline-routes contract updated accordingly. The processor's existing transaction lifecycle, event emission, and retry logic all apply unchanged.

## Downstream injection (stages 2-7)

### Renderer module (`backend/src/llm/prep/vocabulary-renderer.ts`)

Pure deterministic function — no LLM cost, no I/O:

```typescript
export function renderVocabularyRegistry(
  registry: VocabularyRegistry,
): string {
  if (registry.entries.length === 0 && registry.adjacent_franchises.length === 0) {
    return 'No registered vocabulary for this world.'
  }
  const lines: string[] = []
  if (registry.adjacent_franchises.length > 0) {
    lines.push(
      `Adjacent franchises whose iconic vocabulary you must AVOID in your output: ${registry.adjacent_franchises.join(', ')}.`,
    )
  }
  if (registry.entries.length > 0) {
    lines.push("This world's distinctive vocabulary:")
    for (const entry of registry.entries) {
      const avoidance = entry.avoids ? ` (use instead of "${entry.avoids}")` : ''
      const usage = entry.usage_notes ? ` — ${entry.usage_notes}` : ''
      lines.push(`- "${entry.term}"${avoidance}: ${entry.definition}${usage}`)
    }
  }
  return lines.join('\n')
}
```

Example output for Veil & Chalk:

```
Adjacent franchises whose iconic vocabulary you must AVOID in your output: Harry Potter.

This world's distinctive vocabulary:
- "the unwitched" (use instead of "muggle"): non-magical people; this world's term for those without arcane sight — always lowercase, never preceded by 'mere' or 'common'
- "the Concordat of Concealment" (use instead of "Statute of Secrecy"): the law forbidding magical revelation to the unwitched
- "the Ash Network" (use instead of "Floo network"): the magical transport system using warded fireplaces
- ...
```

### Wiring each stage handler

Each of stages 2-7 (`world-and-factions.ts`, `locations-and-npcs.ts`, `entity-relationships.ts`, `starting-situation.ts`, `random-tables.ts`, `coherence-pass.ts`) loads the registry from the world row and renders it. The current code already loads world data per-stage; the registry just gets added to the SELECT. The rendered prose lands in a new `{{vocabularyRegistry}}` placeholder in each stage's prompt template — at the top of the system context, before stage-specific instructions:

```
You are a world-building assistant for a tabletop D&D 5e campaign...

## World Vocabulary

{{vocabularyRegistry}}

## Campaign Summary
...

## Your Task
...
```

The substitution code per stage:

```typescript
const world = await loadWorld(client, worldId)
const vocabularyPrompt = renderVocabularyRegistry(world.vocabulary_registry)
const prompt = PROMPT_TEMPLATE
  .replace('{{worldName}}', world.name)
  .replace('{{concept}}', world.concept)
  .replace('{{vocabularyRegistry}}', vocabularyPrompt)
```

### Anthropic prompt caching

The rendered registry is stable across a single world's stages — perfect cache candidate. Stages 2-7 set `cacheControl: { type: 'ephemeral' }` on the system-prompt prefix that includes `{{vocabularyRegistry}}`. First downstream stage pays the cache *write*; subsequent stages pay cache *reads* (10% of normal price).

Per-world cached portion: ~2K tokens (20-entry registry × ~100 tokens). Cost across stages 2-7: cache write ($0.006) + 5 cache reads ($0.0006 each) ≈ **$0.009/world**.

### Stages do NOT modify the registry mid-flight

Stages 2-7 are consumers. They don't write to `vocabulary_registry`. They don't IP-scan their own output (that's the future backstop bead). They don't reject or retry based on registry compliance — the positive constraint + avoidance reminders in the system prompt are the load-bearing mechanism; if a model still leaks IP, the consolidation pass + future backstop scan catch it.

Stages 2-7 see ONLY the stage 1.5 registry. The consolidation pass runs AFTER stages 2-7 (after `character_creation` phase), so its appends do not retroactively constrain world-gen prose. This is intentional: consolidation is for the player-facing glossary surface and for the future Fast Thinker / Slow Thinker / Scribe live-play hot path (which will load the latest registry at each turn), not for retroactive constraint of world-gen output.

## Post-character-creation stage: `vocabulary_consolidation`

> **DEFERRED to a follow-on bead.** The hook point this stage depends on — completion of a `character_creation` processor — doesn't exist in the codebase yet (slated for MYT-52). Rather than build the hook stub speculatively, this stage is fully designed here for the follow-on bead to pick up when the precondition lands. Everything below documents the intended shape; no code lands for this stage in the myth-pfke implementation. Section retained verbatim so the follow-on bead can implement directly from this spec.

(Not numbered alongside stages 1-7 — `character_creation` is a phase, not a numbered world-gen stage, so this is the consolidation pass that runs after that phase completes.)

### Purpose

The predictive stage 1.5 covers what the model can anticipate from the concept alone. But stages 2-7 generate distinctive coined terms in-fiction (NPC honorifics, faction internal jargon, named historical events) and character creation introduces character-specific or faction-specific jargon. The consolidation pass is the *recall* pass: read everything that was generated, identify distinctive terms not yet in the registry, append them.

### Placement

```
... stage 7: coherence_pass
              ↓
phase: world_snapshot_ready  (existing, from myth-exac)
              ↓
character_creation phase     (existing; creates campaign_characters)
              ↓
CONSOLIDATION: vocabulary_consolidation  ← NEW
              ↓
phase: world_ready / complete
```

### Prompt template (`backend/src/llm/prompts/prep/vocabulary-consolidation.txt`)

Sketch:

```
You are extending a world-vocabulary registry for a tabletop D&D 5e
campaign. The world has been generated (regions, factions, NPCs, prose)
and characters have been created. The existing registry was built
predictively from the concept alone. Your job: identify NEW distinctive
terms that surfaced during generation or character creation but aren't
yet in the registry, and append them.

## Existing registry (terms already defined — DO NOT re-emit)

{{existingTerms}}

## World snapshot (entities, facts, prose kernels)

{{worldSnapshot}}

## Character backstories

{{characterBackstories}}

## Your Task

Identify NEW distinctive coined terms that:

- Appeared in the world snapshot or character backstories.
- Are NOT already in the existing registry.
- Are distinctive enough to belong in a player-facing glossary.

Skip:

- Terms already in the registry.
- D&D 5e SRD-canonical mechanics: spells, classes, races, magic schools.
- Generic fantasy nouns: wand, sword, dragon, witch, wizard.
- Boring proper nouns: every NPC name, every minor village.
- Variations or near-synonyms of existing registry terms.

For each new entry, populate `term`, `definition`, optionally `avoids`,
and optionally `usage_notes`, using the same conventions as the predictive
pass.

If nothing new is worth glossary-izing, return an empty array.

Respond with ONLY the JSON object matching this shape:

{
  "new_entries": [
    {
      "term": "string",
      "definition": "string",
      "avoids": "string (optional)",
      "usage_notes": "string (optional)"
    },
    ...
  ]
}
```

### Handler (`backend/src/llm/prep/stages/vocabulary-consolidation.ts`)

Loads the world snapshot (entities, facts, prose_kernels, relationships) and character backstories. Renders the existing registry's `term` list compactly into `{{existingTerms}}`. Renders the snapshot and characters into reasonably-bounded prose blobs. Calls `generateObject` on Haiku (per cost considerations — this is a recall + filter task, not creative generation). Stamps `source: 'consolidation'` and `access_level: 'known'` on each new entry. Merges append-only: existing registry entries are preserved verbatim; new entries are appended; defense-in-depth rejection of any new entry whose `term` collides with an existing term (the prompt instructs append-only, but code enforces).

Cost: ~3K input tokens (snapshot summary + characters + existing registry terms) + ~1K output tokens at Haiku rates ≈ **$0.005/world**.

### Failure modes

Same as stage 1.5. If the consolidation call fails terminally, the world is still usable — the predictive registry from stage 1.5 is sufficient for downstream play (downstream stages 2-7 have already received their injection; the consolidation is purely additive). The processor should mark the consolidation stage failed in `pipeline_runs` but NOT roll back the world; the world progresses to `complete` with the stage 1.5 registry only. (This is a softer failure mode than stages 2-7, which are load-bearing for the world's content.)

## UI surfacing

### World-snapshot endpoint

`GET /api/campaigns/:campaignId/world-snapshot` — extend the `WorldSummary` projection's allowlist to include `vocabulary_registry`. Apply the same `access_level` filter as entities/facts (per myth-exac):

- Default request (no bypass): return only entries with `access_level === 'known'`. Strip `discoverable` and `secret` entries.
- Owner-with-bypass: return all entries regardless of tier.

The endpoint must filter at the API layer, not in the database, because the JSON document is a single column. The filter is a simple `array.filter` on the `entries` array. The `adjacent_franchises` field is always returned (it's metadata about the world's IP-adjacency, not a player-secret).

### Dev-tool world-snapshot panel

The `backend/dev-tool/src/components/world-snapshot-panel.ts` panel gains a new "Glossary" section, rendered below the entity tree:

- Section header: "Glossary"
- Subheader (if non-empty): `Adjacent franchises: {comma-separated list}`
- Entries rendered as `<dl>` (definition list): `<dt>{term}</dt><dd>{definition}{avoids: "(use instead of X)"}{usage_notes: " — {notes}"}</dd>`
- Entries grouped by `source` (foldable groups: "From world creation (12)", "From consolidation (5)", "From play (0)" — the last hidden until Scribe runtime ships).
- Tier badges (`known` / `discoverable` / `secret`) shown next to each entry when bypass is active.
- Empty state: "No vocabulary registered for this world."

The panel reads from the existing world-snapshot SSE channel (no new event types). The registry is delivered as part of the initial snapshot; the snapshot endpoint includes it.

## Forward-compatibility

Declared in this design; no code written for these surfaces in this scope:

### Fast Thinker / Slow Thinker (multiplayer runtime)

When multiplayer-session-runtime ships, FT/ST will load `worlds.vocabulary_registry` at turn entry, render it via `renderVocabularyRegistry`, and inject it into their system prompts with `cacheControl: 'ephemeral'`. The renderer module is built now; FT/ST just call it. This puts the registry on the live-play hot path with the same caching pattern as stages 2-7.

### Scribe runtime write-back

When Scribe ships, it can append new entries with `source: 'scribe_runtime'` and a runtime-determined `access_level` (potentially `discoverable` or `secret` if the term was introduced as part of a hidden reveal). The Scribe's extraction prompt will likely instruct: "if the just-completed turn introduced a distinctive coined term not in the registry, emit it as a new entry." The data shape, append-only semantics, source tag, and access_level field are all designed to support this.

### Multiplayer wiki UI

The user-facing wiki section will render the registry as a Glossary page, with `known`/`discoverable` filtering applied per access tier (mirrors the world-snapshot endpoint's logic). Pure UI work, no new data needed.

### Future backstop Haiku IP-scan

The backstop scan reads the registry's `avoids` values + `adjacent_franchises` list and checks generated text (world-gen stage outputs OR Fast Thinker turns) against them. No new data needed; the scan is the consumer.

## Risks

1. **Model misses an adjacent franchise.** Niche or very-new franchises may not be in the model's training data. The registry then won't include avoidance entries for those terms. **Mitigation:** the registry is the model's best-effort. If a frontier-model knowledge gap surfaces in practice, the build-if-needed bead (curated franchise hints list) gets activated.

2. **Model invents bad vocabulary.** The model might emit terms that don't fit the world's tone or that read as clunky. **Mitigation:** stage 1.5 uses Sonnet (not Haiku) precisely because vocabulary generation benefits from the larger model's creative judgment. Quality regression would surface in user-facing wiki glossaries; this is the kind of thing that needs an eval harness, not a smoke.

3. **Downstream stages ignore the constraint.** The positive constraint + avoidance reminders are not a hard guarantee. A model could still emit "muggle" in stage 2 prose despite the registry. **Mitigation:** the future backstop Haiku scan is the hard guarantee; this design lays the data shape it needs. Until the backstop ships, the constraint is best-effort. Given the model's compliance with positive constraints in our other prompt-engineered stages, this is acceptable for v1.

4. **Cost overhead.** Stage 1.5 + the consolidation stage add ~$0.05/world to world-gen cost. Cached injection adds ~$0.01. Total ~$0.06/world. **Mitigation:** within budget — current world-gen averages ~$0.84/world end-to-end; this is ~7% overhead for the IP-defense + glossary feature. Tracked in `cost-cap.ts`.

5. **Registry grows unbounded over a campaign's lifetime.** With future Scribe runtime write-back, the registry could grow indefinitely. **Mitigation:** out of scope for this design; size cap + eviction policy is a future bead. The 60-entry cap on `VocabularyRegistrySchema` is a hard limit that would surface the issue before it becomes ops-level.

6. **Consolidation pass appends low-value terms.** The model might over-eagerly add entries that aren't actually distinctive (every NPC name, every villager's nickname). **Mitigation:** the consolidation prompt is explicit about quality bar ("worth glossary-izing" / "distinctive enough"). Quality regression is an eval-harness concern; the 15-entry cap on `VocabularyConsolidationOutputSchema.new_entries` bounds worst-case bloat.

7. **Smoke flakiness.** The real-LLM smoke depends on Sonnet/Haiku consistently identifying HP as adjacent for the Veil & Chalk prompt. If the model varies, the smoke could fail intermittently. **Mitigation:** the smoke asserts loose properties (at least one entry has an `avoids` populated with one of the four HP-iconic terms, NOT a specific one). If the smoke proves flaky in practice, narrow the smoke's assertions or move to an eval harness for the IP-identification quality property.
